01 · The big picture

What is SURPS?

SURPS is not fundamentally about hit points, dice sizes, damage formulas, or character classes. It is a shared language for describing entities and the circumstances in which they act. It asks what is true, what is possible, what is attempted, what resists, and what changes.

Everything is situated

An action is not an isolated number. It depends on the actor, intent, target, environment, time, capability, and opposition.

Consequences follow causes

A miss, a hit, or a critical outcome is not merely a label. It changes what happens next, including things beyond the immediate target.

02 · Two adapters

One source. Two experiences.

The underlying behavior is authored once. During creation and workshop preparation, SURPS evaluates representative conditions and derives the tabletop procedures. The digital adapter retains the detailed model and executes it with machine assistance.

TABLETOP ADAPTER

A human-scale expression

The player declares intent and method. The referee uses a compact procedure, a small number of dice, and qualitative outcome bands derived from the behavior model.

At the table: resolve uncertainty with the prepared procedure, then describe the actual consequence in the fiction.

Workshop responsibilities

Sample representative actor, target, equipment, environment, and opposition states. Compare outcome distributions. Generate and version the dice procedure, quick-reference tables, and qualitative effect bands. Review edge cases before publishing the paper adapter.

SHARED SEMANTICS

Same intent, same causal meaning

The adapters must agree about what the action means and what its outcomes change. The paper version compresses detail; it must not invent a different reality.

Invariant: the paper adapter is a projection of the behavior model, not the authority that defines the behavior model.

Every derived value records the source behavior version, simulation/provider version, calibration envelope, assumptions, sample set, and generation version. A changed source invalidates the derived artifact.

03 · Travel is a system

Travel is more than a movement roll.

A journey emerges from a route, terrain, weather, load, pace, capability, time, and the changing condition of the traveler. The picture below is a conceptual teaching illustration: a route is a sequence of environments and decisions, not a single distance number.

Player sees

The route, visible hazards, expected effort, available choices, and what their character can reasonably perceive.

Game Master can inspect

Terrain profiles, weather exposure, load and pace, route dependencies, hidden hazards, time costs, capability checks, and consequence triggers.

05 · The missing seam

Compile in the workshop. Execute with prepared behavior.

The expensive work belongs in authoring, validation, simulation, and compilation—not repeatedly in the middle of play. But we must cache the right things.

Author the behavior.
Store the source model, assumptions, units, provider references, version, and provenance in the repository.
Validate and simulate representative cases.
Use domain-owned evaluators to explore the declared operating envelope, edge cases, and outcome distributions.
Generate both adapters.
Emit the tabletop dice procedure, tables, and qualitative bands; compile the digital behavior profile, lookup structures, and diagnostics from the same source revision.
Cache immutable compiled artifacts.
Key them by behavior content hash, compiler version, provider version, units/schema version, and declared settings. Warm them when the experience loads.
Evaluate live state cheaply.
At play time, bind current actor, target, equipment, environment, and time state to the compiled model. Recompute what changed; do not rerun the workshop sweep.
Cache invariant: cache the compiled behavior and reusable response data—not a final outcome that depends on mutable world state. If an actor is injured, a door closes, the wind changes, or a target moves, the live evaluation must see that change.
06 · How to read a SURPS book

Start with the world. Then learn its parts.

Player path

A short overview of the concept, what the character can perceive and attempt, the choices available, and how to participate. Avoid forcing players to read implementation detail.

Game Master path

Deep domain data, causal relationships, hidden facts, tools for changing the scene, outcome examples, and diagnostics. Let the GM inspect and manipulate the world without carrying all of it in working memory.

Paper path

A complete, human-usable procedure derived from the underlying model. Include printable aids, dice instructions, qualitative outcomes, and worked examples.

Digital path

The detailed behavior model, provider contracts, typed inputs, live state, cached artifacts, traces, and visualizations. Show exactly what is implemented versus illustrative or planned.

One world. One authored behavior. Multiple honest ways to experience it.
